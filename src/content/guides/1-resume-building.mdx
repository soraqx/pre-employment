## Meet the ATS

An **Applicant Tracking System**, or ATS, is software that helps employers collect, organize, and review job applications. Before your resume reaches a recruiter, an ATS may scan it for details such as your skills, work experience, education, and keywords related to the role.

That may sound a little intimidating, but dochi has good news: an ATS-friendly resume is simply a resume that is clear, organized, and easy to read. No fancy tricks needed.

## Formatting Rules

Keep your resume tidy enough for both a computer and a busy recruiter to understand.

- Use familiar headings such as **Work Experience**, **Education**, and **Skills**.
- Choose a readable font and keep the body text at a comfortable size.
- Use a simple, single-column layout whenever possible.
- Avoid complex tables, text boxes, charts, and decorative graphics.
- Use standard bullet points instead of icons to describe your achievements.
- Keep dates and job titles formatted consistently.
- Put important information in the main document, not in headers or footers.

Think of it like clearing a path for a curious cat: fewer obstacles make it easier to reach the important details.

## Keywords

Keywords help the ATS connect your resume with the job description. Read the posting carefully and notice the skills, tools, qualifications, and responsibilities it repeats.

- Use the employer's exact wording when it honestly matches your experience.
- Include both the full term and abbreviation when helpful, such as **Customer Relationship Management (CRM)**.
- Add keywords naturally inside your experience and skills sections.
- Never copy every keyword or claim a skill you do not have.

Your resume should still sound like you. The goal is clarity, not keyword stuffing.

## One Last Paw Check

Proofread your contact details, check every date, and compare the final resume with the job posting. A clean and honest resume gives recruiters a comfortable place to pause and learn what you can do.
